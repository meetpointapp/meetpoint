# Derinkuyu — long video script (EN, Brian) — draft 1

Title (search): Derinkuyu: The Underground City That Hid 20,000 People
Target: 8–9 min, chapters. Each line = one visual beat (no image used twice).

## 0. Cold open (0:00–0:40)
1. In 1963, a man in central Turkey knocked down a wall in his basement.
2. Behind it, there was a draft of cold air... and a dark passage going down.
3. He followed it. And it kept going. Down. And down. And down.
4. What he had found was not a cellar. It was a city.
5. At least eight levels deep, with room for twenty thousand people... and their animals.
6. And it had been hidden right under everyone's feet for centuries.
7. This is Derinkuyu. And the real question is not how they built it... but what they were so afraid of.

## 1. The discovery (0:40–1:40)
8. Derinkuyu is a small town in Cappadocia, in the heart of Anatolia.
9. The ground here is volcanic tuff: ash from ancient eruptions, pressed into soft stone.
10. You can carve it with simple iron tools. And when air touches it, it slowly hardens.
11. The local story says the man's chickens kept disappearing into a crack in the wall.
12. Whatever the reason, when he broke through, he found tunnel after tunnel.
13. Archaeologists came. They cleared rubble that had sealed the passages for decades.
14. Six years later, in 1969, the city opened to visitors.
15. And even today, only part of it can be visited.

## 2. Going down (1:40–3:20)
16. Let's go down, level by level.
17. Near the surface: stables. Animals lived closest to the light.
18. Then rooms for living and sleeping, carved straight out of the rock.
19. Kitchens, with ceilings still black from smoke.
20. Wine and oil presses. Storage rooms for grain.
21. Wells, so the city never had to come up for water.
22. Wider halls where people could gather, study, or pray.
23. And near the bottom, a cross-shaped room, which many believe was a church.
24. The deepest point reaches around eighty-five meters.
25. Deeper than a twenty-story building... all dug by hand.
26. Getting between levels means narrow, steep stairs. Often you have to bend almost in half.
27. That was not bad design. It was the point.

## 3. Built for a siege (3:20–5:00)
28. Because Derinkuyu was not built for comfort. It was built to survive an attack.
29. Look at these: huge round stones, like millstones, standing next to the doorways.
30. Each one weighs hundreds of kilograms.
31. When danger came, people rolled them across the passage... from the inside.
32. Each level could be sealed on its own.
33. From outside, there was no way to move them. No handle. Nothing to grab.
34. The corridors are narrow, so attackers had to come one at a time, bent over, in the dark.
35. And breathing was solved too: dozens of ventilation shafts run from the surface to the deepest floors.
36. Some of these shafts also reached groundwater, working as wells.
37. Fresh air, water, food, animals... a whole town could disappear underground for weeks.

## 4. Who built it? (5:00–6:40)
38. So who started digging? Nobody knows for sure.
39. Some researchers point to the Phrygians, around the eighth to seventh century BC.
40. Around 400 BC, the Greek writer Xenophon described people in Anatolia living in underground houses with their animals.
41. Later, in the Byzantine era, these cities grew bigger.
42. Christians of the region hid here during raids and wars, for hundreds of years.
43. Each generation dug a little deeper, a little wider.
44. And Derinkuyu is not alone. Cappadocia has many underground cities.
45. One of them, Kaymaklı, is about eight kilometers away. Some believe a tunnel once connected the two.
46. If that's true... this was not a single shelter. It was an underground network.

## 5. The last people (6:40–7:40)
47. People used these tunnels until the twentieth century.
48. In 1923, after the population exchange between Turkey and Greece, the Greek Orthodox families of Derinkuyu left.
49. The town above changed. The tunnels below fell silent.
50. Passages filled with dust and rubble... and the city was forgotten.
51. Until that one wall came down in 1963.
52. Since 1985, the area has been part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site: Göreme National Park and the rock sites of Cappadocia.

## 6. Ending (7:40–8:30)
53. Today, you can walk down the same stairs where families once waited in the dark.
54. You can touch the stone doors they rolled shut, and listen.
55. Imagine twenty thousand people, holding their breath, while someone searches the fields above.
56. Derinkuyu was built by fear. But it kept people alive.
57. And here's what still keeps archaeologists awake: large parts of it have never been fully explored.
58. If you could go down one more level... would you?
59. Tell me in the comments. And if you want the next lost place in Turkey, you know what to do.
