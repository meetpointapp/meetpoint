BASLIK: The World's Oldest Shipwreck Was Just Found in Turkey?
ACIKLAMA:
Off Kekova Island in southern Turkey, divers found a ship that sank 4,400–4,700 years ago, around the time the Great Pyramid was built. More than 30 clay jars and 3 stone anchors still lie 30 meters down. The excavation team says it may be the oldest known shipwreck; the claim is still being studied.

Opening and museum scenes: Kekova coast, an example Bronze Age jar, and the Uluburun wreck replica (Bodrum Museum), labeled on screen.

BASLIK: Was Ajax's Grave Just Found Near Troy?
ACIKLAMA:
Ancient writers placed the grave of Ajax, the Trojan War hero, on the coast near Rhoiteion, close to Troy. Archaeologists have now described a mound there with rows of stones sealed with clay, hidden under soil from a WWII bunker. Its age is still unknown, so the link to Ajax is not proven.

Opening image: 1804 engraving of a burial mound near Rhoiteion. Ajax & Achilles: Exekias amphora, c. 530 BC (Vatican Museums).

=== 23 KASIM PAZARTESI 10:00 ===

BASLIK: 11,000 Years Ago, Someone Tried to Invent Pottery
ACIKLAMA:
At Çemka Höyük in Mardin, Turkey, archaeologists studied 46 fragments of fired clay from at least 9 objects, dated to around 11,000 years ago — some of the earliest fired-clay containers in Southwest Asia. Different shapes and clay mixes show people experimenting long before pottery became common (Kodaş et al., Antiquity 2026).

Museum scenes are examples of Neolithic finds from the Şanlıurfa Museum, labeled on screen.

BASLIK: This Door Only Closes From the Inside
ACIKLAMA:
80 meters under Derinkuyu in central Turkey, every level of the underground city could be sealed with a rolling stone door weighing hundreds of kilograms, and only from the inside. Full documentary on the channel.

BASLIK: How Did 20,000 People Breathe 85 m Underground?
ACIKLAMA:
More than 50 ventilation shafts run from the surface of Derinkuyu down to its deepest floors. Fresh air sinks, stale air rises, and some shafts doubled as wells. Full documentary on the channel.
